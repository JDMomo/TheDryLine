# The Dry Line — Story Bible

## Logline

The sea withdrew, the evacuation ended, and everyone left the railway behind. The railway did not know that.

Decades later, the Dry Line still follows its final emergency timetable. Four Wardens continue collecting fare and routing phantom passengers. At the Terminus, the Long Clock keeps the order valid because the cancellation punch never arrived. The player carries that unfinished act of bureaucracy down the line.

The central dramatic idea is **a public system that outlived the people it was built to serve**. The Wardens are not villains. They are machines trapped inside valid instructions.

## Player role

The Wanderer is not a chosen hero and does not begin with complete knowledge. They find the Last Timetable in the Junction and realize that one service remains legally “running.” Every gate is open because the emergency order superseded ordinary closures. The run becomes an investigation: follow any line, discover what failed, and decide how much of the network to release before confronting the Long Clock.

Mechanically, this supports the existing open-order run. The player may fight the Long Clock immediately, but every Warden left active lends one attack to it. Narrative understanding and mechanical preparation therefore point in the same direction without forcing a linear sequence.

## Reveal order

1. **The Junction — The Last Timetable.** Five gates are open. Four local Wardens still enforce fares; the fifth line goes to the authority that keeps their orders alive.
2. **Wreck Quay — The evacuation really happened.** Freight markings were overwritten with passenger priority. The cancellation stamp is missing.
3. **Signal Reef — The message failed in transit.** Signal lamps have repeated the same departure code for years. They were trying to relay the cancellation toward the Terminus.
4. **Salt Cutting — The disaster was fast.** Survey marks show the sea withdrew in nine days. The railway was temporarily ordered to move people rather than goods.
5. **Ticket Hall — The actual document.** The final departure remains uncancelled under Long Clock authority. The original cancellation punch is found behind the Gatewhale.
6. **The Terminus — The machine is obeying correctly.** A maintenance slate explains that the Long Clock cannot disregard a valid timetable. The cancellation must be delivered physically.
7. **Ending.** The final order is punched. Lamps extinguish down the line. The silence is not destruction; it is permission to stop.

## The Wardens

### Tollkeeper — Wreck Quay
Purpose: account for passage and freight.  
Tragedy: its ledger says every evacuation passenger passed, then ends on “Await cancellation.”  
Fight language: charges, toll enforcement, ledger-like fare slots.  
After defeat: it releases its ledger rather than “dying.”

### Lamp-Heron — Signal Reef
Purpose: relay signals through the dead coral towers.  
Tragedy: it has transmitted the same unresolved code for years.  
After defeat: the network answers once — **RECEIVED — TOO LATE**.

### Switchman Idol — Salt Cutting
Purpose: keep routes mutually safe through the cutting.  
Tragedy: it still sees evacuation traffic that no longer exists and refuses conflicting routes.  
After defeat: its lenses settle toward the Junction, effectively routing survivors home.

### Gatewhale — Ticket Hall
Purpose: validate tickets and protect the departure platforms.  
Tragedy: it possesses the physical cancellation punch but was never authorized to use it.  
After defeat: the punch becomes available to the party.

### Long Clock — Terminus
Purpose: certify the timetable and coordinate every line.  
Tragedy: it is the most correct machine on the railway. Its order is valid, so service must continue.  
Fight line: **TIMETABLE VALID. SERVICE CONTINUES.**  
Mechanical meaning: it borrows attacks from every Warden whose order is still active.

## Regional emotional progression

- **Wreck Quay:** abandonment. Human work stopped mid-task.
- **Signal Reef:** repetition. Messages continue after listeners are gone.
- **Salt Cutting:** evidence. The landscape itself records how quickly the world changed.
- **Ticket Hall:** bureaucracy. The exact administrative failure becomes visible.
- **Terminus:** responsibility. Nothing is malfunctioning; someone must finally finish the job.

## Tone rules

- Quiet melancholy over horror. No undead, plague, gothic religion, or insect kingdom language.
- Machines are specific pieces of railway/public infrastructure, not generic evil robots.
- Keep text short in play. One or two sentences should imply a larger history.
- Avoid exposition that tells the player what to feel.
- Humor may come from stubborn machinery and obsolete procedure, never from mocking the disaster.
- “Defeating” a Warden should increasingly read as releasing it from an obsolete order.
- The ending should feel like relief and earned stillness, not an explosion or conquest.

## Delivery rules

Story is delivered through:
- first-entry regional lines;
- a handful of authored discoveries in important rooms;
- Warden order lines and aftermath;
- changes in the Junction after each Warden;
- the Route Board's current objective/latest note;
- the end screen epilogue.

Do not add long cutscenes unless a future playtest proves the player is missing the premise. Control should almost never be taken away for story.
